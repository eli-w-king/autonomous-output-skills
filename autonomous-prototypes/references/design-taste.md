# Design taste

The user's visual, motion and layout taste, as it has held across prototypes, variation labs and
website work. Use it for every polish pass and paste the notes that apply into lab and agent briefs.
These are defaults, not universal law: an explicit instruction in the current request wins, and the
user's latest word beats anything here.

## Motion

- **Motion follows the scroll.** A page that scrolls vertically moves things vertically. No
  sideways panning or horizontal scrolling sections, and movement in one axis at a time, never
  diagonal. Text scrolls with the page and never parks in one spot; the demo beside it may hold
  still.
- **One change after another.** Sequence changes so the eye can follow them; never change many
  things at once.
- **Light and smooth.** Gradual, continuous transitions instead of sudden fades or pop-ins, short
  travel, no flashing, and nothing slow or laggy. Ambient motion beats busy motion.

## Layout and type

- **No centered text.** Left-aligned copy, titles that are not oversized or overly bold, and the
  same on phones, where large centered text turns illegible.
- **Align to the grid.** Headings, descriptions, buttons and demos line up with the layout's columns
  and page margins, with consistent spacing between them.
- **One accent and the page's own type scale.** No new sizes without a reason, and nothing competes
  with the hero.

## Fit the product

- **Premium and minimal.** Contemporary and sparse, with plenty of white space, so it looks like a
  well designed product. Glow and glass only with restraint: nothing that reads as science fiction,
  and no gimmicks like confetti.
- **Native and restrained.** It looks like the product it lives in: real hover, selection and glyphs
  instead of generic web widgets, a small footprint, and nothing that looks out of place.
- **Embedded beats layered.** Details open on the page or in the thing itself: no modals, scrims,
  floating cards or camera zooms.
- **Whole things only.** On product pages and in labs, tiles, windows and demos are never cropped,
  resized or shrunk to fit, and never look pasted on top of the layout they land in. Promo art is
  the exception (below).
- **The design explains itself.** No on-screen text explaining an interaction.

## Content

- **Grown-up and safe.** Nothing childish, cartoony or cute (serious beats cute), and no sensitive
  flows (sign-in, purchases, personal data) in product demos.

## Promo art

- **The real product is the art.** Event covers, invites and banners show the real product UI: a
  wall of real demo tiles or UI widgets in two or three columns, some bleeding off the frame's
  edges, rather than mockups or abstract illustration.

## Maintaining this file

The weekly skill miner adds taste that recurs across sessions. Keep entries short, generic and
free of project names, and never use em or en dashes.
